## Arc contract

- **County:** Maigh Eo / Mayo
- **Flagship:** Gráinne Ní Mháille, broader life organised around the 1593 crisis
- **Dramatic question:** Can she preserve her family, authority, and way of life as
  Tudor power closes around Clew Bay?
- **Shape:** nine chapters; Story mode targets 60–90 minutes measured by in-app
  walkthrough, not word count
- **Mode integrity (D21):** one authored page sequence per chapter, filtered into
  Story and Learning. Story mode carries the complete account with no language gate and
  opens the next county without claiming the 20 words. Learning mode keeps the full
  causal chain plus every required exercise, turns the county gold, and moves the words
  into *Words you carry*.
- **Completion artifact:** learner-authored voyage chart (Clew Bay → London → Mayo)
- **Next county:** authored by spine after Mayo gold (not chosen here)

The proven Rockfleet chapter (`mayo.rockfleet`, 24 pages) is **Chapter 3** of this arc.
It is the exercise-rich consolidation anchor and the only chapter already implemented.
See the open decision on its word introductions below.

## Per-chapter storyboard

Columns follow the predecessor: historical change · human stake · evidence that
reveals it · Irish earned (I = introduced here, R = reused) · learner action. Claim IDs
reference the ledger in the source brief.

### Chapter 1 — Clew Bay and Umhaill
- **Historical change:** Umhaill read as a maritime polity — islands, fleet routes,
  shore castles, tolls and trade — not a romantic free sea. *(C01, C03 background)*
- **Human stake:** if this order falls, Mayo's Atlantic edge becomes someone else's coast.
- **Evidence:** coast/island geography; Sidney's "feminine sea captain" recognition as
  one overheard official beat *(C04, L1 paraphrase only)*; period map territory at
  closer look.
- **Irish:** **I** *farraige, bá, áit, as* (origin scaffold *as … mé*).
- **Learner action:** trace the bay mouth and islands; hear the place words; say a
  Mayo-origin line (*as … mé*). Full-sentence work begins here (meets the "by Chapter 2"
  standard early).
- **Do not:** catalogue every raid; invent childhood dialogue; lead with "pirate queen"
  *(C18 excluded as fact)*.

### Chapter 2 — Marriage, kin, and alliances
- **Historical change:** authority is shown to rest on marriages, children and
  followers as much as on boats. *(C05 marriages; C06 children; C07 base)*
- **Human stake:** the family is the survival system — and the part an enemy can hold.
- **Evidence:** interrogatory answers naming parents, marriages, children *(C05, C06)*;
  kept at the level the papers support, without inventing domestic scenes.
- **Irish:** **I** *teaghlach, mac, deartháir, bean*; **R** *áit* (a place that holds a
  family).
- **Learner action:** assign people to the *teaghlach*; use *mac* / *deartháir* in a
  belonging line; meet *bean* as a period recognition of a woman of power (no shame
  frame, per weave stress-test).
- **Do not:** stage the battlement "divorce" as fact *(C17 afterlife/exclude)*; assert
  a fixed marriage chronology the secondary literature disputes.

### Chapter 3 — Rockfleet *(proven — already implemented)*
- **Historical change:** castle, inlet, boats and household read as one connected
  system; the record's limits kept visible. *(C07 place; C06 household; C08 pressure seed)*
- **Human stake:** family and harbour are the same survival system; pressure on one
  point reaches all of them.
- **Evidence:** Rockfleet as place *(C07)*; household framed from 1593 naming *(C06)*;
  `SP 63/170` as a state paper shown as evidence.
- **Irish:** **I** *caisleán*; **R** *teaghlach, mac, bean* (see open decision **D-B**).
- **Learner action:** enter Rockfleet; keep people and place distinct; build and type
  the *Tá … anseo* location frame; ungraded speaking; delayed retrieval.
- **Status:** implemented as `mayo.rockfleet` (10 Story pages, 18 Learning pages, 12
  exercises). Treat as the exercise-rich anchor; trim per open decision **D-C**.

### Chapter 4 — Power at sea
- **Historical change:** she maintained herself and her people by land and sea,
  including opportunistic seizure and tolling alongside trade and pragmatic alliance —
  described without sanitising into pure "trade." *(C03; C04 galleys/men; C08 leverage)*
- **Human stake:** a working livelihood that is real, contested, and dependent on the
  system Ch3 assembled.
- **Evidence:** her 1593 self-account *(C03)*; Sidney's galley/men recognition at
  L2 *(C04, verify exact cite before final)*; hostile official descriptions kept visibly
  biased *(C08, L2)*.
- **Irish:** **I** *long*; **R** *farraige, bá, áit* in an active-production context
  (the fleet works the sea, the bay, named places).
- **Learner action:** build sentences that put the fleet to work across reused place
  words; sequence how sea power actually functioned.
- **Note:** this chapter is a **reuse-and-consolidation** chapter for vocabulary (only
  *long* is new). That is deliberate — no chapter exists primarily to teach words — but
  see open decision **D-D** on whether a reserve headword (*saor*, *fan*, *cabhair*)
  should replace a weaker slot to give Ch4 its own new word.

### Chapter 5 — Bingham closes in
- **Historical change:** one continuous pressure movement — Bingham's presidency
  intensifies; Tibbott imprisoned and Donal held; fleet leverage and maintenance lost.
  *(C08, C10; C09 son Owen's death only at closer look)*
- **Human stake:** Tibbott's life, Donal's freedom, and her remaining authority.
- **Evidence:** hostile Bingham framing vs her maintenance plea as a paired-voice beat;
  imprisonment as the tipping fact. **No 1584–92 year-by-year tour.** *(C09 kept in
  closer look only, not asserted as settled murder on L1.)*
- **Irish:** **I** *caill* (at the moment of loss); **R** *long* (fleet seized), *mac*
  (Tibbott held), *deartháir* (Donal held).
- **Learner action:** compare two voices on the same coast; produce *caill* at the
  squeeze; feel the kin words return under pressure.
- **Do not:** make the learner "defeat" Bingham; romanticise rebellion; dump a timeline.

### Chapter 6 — The road to London
- **Historical change:** local power can no longer resolve the pressure; the petition
  to Elizabeth becomes the remaining path, which demands she become legible to the
  Tudor state. *(C11 petition; C12 court process)*
- **Human stake:** to be heard at all, she must submit to English court process.
- **Evidence:** the July 1593 petition — maintenance plea plus offer to serve against
  the Queen's enemies — as crafted self-presentation *(C11, L1 paraphrase in source
  brief)*; Thames/Greenwich as arrival image without invented dialogue *(C12 close)*.
- **Irish:** **I** *iarr* (the remaining verb), *téigh* (toward London), *tar* (arrival
  into the court world); **R** *caill* (loss forces the ask). Motion pair *téigh / tar*.
- **Learner action:** decide the dramatic need to *iarr*; choose *téigh* toward court;
  use *tar* for arrival.
- **Do not:** invent the audience conversation; teach an evidence taxonomy; lead with
  caveats.

### Chapter 7 — Gráinne in the record
- **Historical change:** eighteen articles of interrogatory fix her anglicised name,
  parents, marriages, children, lands and maintenance in the state's papers. *(C11;
  documentary encounter is `SP 63/170`)*
- **Human stake:** the papers hold her name and family in English hands; finding them
  changes who she is to the learner.
- **Evidence:** **the name-find climax** — `SP 63/170 f. 201` interrogatory, calendar
  heading "Grany Ne Malley"; f. 202 answers *(C11; rights cleared for free educational
  use per source brief)*.
- **Irish:** **I** *ainm, mise*; **R** *as* (payoff: *as Maigh Eo mé*), *teaghlach /
  mac / deartháir* (family named in the record), *tar* (came into the record).
  Construction climax: *Is mise …*
- **Learner action:** find the calendar-rendered name; read what the questions demand
  and what her answers preserve; answer *Is mise …*; *as Maigh Eo mé*.
- **Do not:** treat the interrogatory as a private diary — it is her voice shaped for
  English paperwork *(source brief L1 note)*.

### Chapter 8 — The royal answer
- **Historical change:** the Queen orders relief and instructs Bingham regarding
  Gráinne and named kin; Bingham resists giving it effect. *(C13 draft; C14 order and
  resistance)*
- **Human stake:** partial preservation on paper against force still on the coast.
- **Evidence:** the 6 September 1593 draft as the royal-response source (transcription,
  no image required) *(C13, Hatfield/HMC path)*; Bingham's delay as the immediate
  consequence *(C14)*.
- **Irish:** **I** *freagair, tabhair*; **R** *iarr* (asked → answered), *mac*
  (maintenance from sons' estates), *caill* (what was not fully restored).
- **Learner action:** handle the order's terms; mark what is *tabhair*-given and what
  Bingham withholds.
- **Do not:** resolve the whole Mayo afterlife here; do not complete the chart yet.

### Chapter 9 — Return and afterlife
- **Historical change:** she must press again by 1595; the present coast still carries
  the story; later tales remember defiance beside the documentary act. *(C14 1595
  pressure; C15 later galley/death soft; C16 afterlife)*
- **Human stake:** the dramatic question stays live in place and memory.
- **Evidence:** condensed 1595 pressure beat *(C14)*; present Rockfleet/Clew Bay
  *(C07, present)*; one affirmative afterlife screen *(C16 reception only; C17 excluded
  as fact)*.
- **Irish:** **I** *cósta, arís*; **R** *bá / áit / caisleán* (present shore, Rockfleet
  today), *farraige*, *iarr* (*iarr arís* — ask again), *ainm / mise* (chart carries
  both names).
- **Learner action:** stand on the *cósta*; compare afterlife with the documentary act;
  complete the voyage chart.
- **Do not:** equate folklore with the September letter; revoke gold with optional
  extras; open a county picker.

## Mode projection notes

Per chapter, the same page sequence is filtered (visibility `both` / `storyOnly` /
`learningOnly`), following the proven Rockfleet pattern:

- **`both`** — the causal spine: setup, stakes, turn, consequence, evidence limit.
  Present in Story and Learning; one shared completion record.
- **`storyOnly`** — extended narrative depth, closer-look evidence, atmosphere. Story
  mode only.
- **`learningOnly`** — the full-screen exercises, in their authored positions after the
  story has made each word meaningful.

Rules carried from the plan: no exercise appears before the story has made its language
meaningful; switching modes moves to the next incomplete visible page without
restarting or granting unearned progress; Story completion opens the route without gold;
Learning completion turns the county gold and activates the 20 words for review.

## Open decisions for the approval gate

These are the choices that change what gets authored. Recommended option first.

- **D-A · Chapter count (9 vs 8 vs 10).** *Recommend 9.* Nine gives each genuine turn
  its own chapter and keeps Story mode in the 60–90 min band without padding. An
  8-chapter cut would merge Ch1+Ch2 (sea + kin) or fold Ch2 into Ch3; a 10th could split
  Ch7 (interrogatory) from a dedicated name-claim chapter. Timing is only provable by
  walkthrough, so the final count may shift ±1 after the first packs are built.

- **D-B · Rockfleet word introductions.** *Recommend reassigning.* The proven pack
  currently *introduces* `caisleán, teaghlach, mac, bean`. In the nine-chapter arc, the
  kin words belong to Chapter 2, so Chapter 3 should introduce only `caisleán` and
  **reuse** `teaghlach, mac, bean`. This is a small edit to the proven pack
  (`introducedLexemeIDs` on the household page + the lifecycle table) and it strengthens
  the lifecycle (kin introduced in the kin chapter, consolidated at Rockfleet). Keeping
  the proven pack frozen instead would force Ch2 to reuse words the learner has not yet
  met — not viable.

- **D-C · Rockfleet exercise density.** *Recommend trimming 12 → ~8.* As a standalone
  proof it carries every mechanic; as Chapter 3 of nine it should stay the richest but
  not blow the county's 30–45 budget. Trim the most redundant items (e.g. one of the two
  location-frame typing/fill exercises) while keeping listen, match, build, type,
  dialogue, comprehension, speaking, and one delayed retrieval.

- **D-D · Chapter 4's new word.** *Recommend keeping `long` as the only new Ch4 word and
  running Ch4 on reuse.* The cleared 20 are place/kin/record-weighted and thin on
  trade/power vocabulary. Alternative: swap a weak slot for a reserve headword (*saor*
  = free, *fan* = stay, *cabhair* = help) to give Ch4 its own new word — but that changes
  the pedagogue-cleared set and needs a fresh lifecycle row, so only if the pedagogue
  prefers it.

- **D-E · Lifecycle "reuse" for final-chapter words.** *Recommend accepting delayed
  retrieval + chart/review as the reuse site for Ch7–9 words.* `freagair, tabhair, arís,
  cósta` (and to a degree `ainm, mise`) are introduced too late to reappear in a *later
  chapter*. The proven pack already operationalises "reuse" as a later-page delayed
  retrieval within the same chapter, plus the voyage-chart consolidation and review
  scheduling. The validator's word-lifecycle check must treat that as satisfying the
  reuse stage for final-chapter words; otherwise the last chapters can never pass.

- **D-F · Historian re-clearance scope.** Chapters 2 and 4 promote previously
  background claims (C05–C07 kin/alliances; C03–C04 power at sea) to chapter weight, and
  C04's galley/men figure needs its exact citation pinned. These need a targeted
  historian pass *before* final prose, not only after. Flagging so the review is
  scheduled at the right point rather than at the end.
