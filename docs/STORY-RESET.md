# Story reset: from inherited chapters to the strongest county stories

*Editorial direction — 11 July 2026. This document records the clean-slate review of
the three built stories and proposes an opening road. It changes the editorial target,
not the shipped JSON: source packets, story briefs, language review, UX design, and
progress migration must precede implementation.*

## The conclusion

The first three chapters proved that the product can make Irish feel tactile,
meaningful, and worth returning to. They did not prove that their inherited subjects
are the best stories for their counties.

| Built county story | Editorial verdict | New centre |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mayo — Breastagh Ogham Stone | Replace as the county flagship | **Gráinne Ní Mháille and the 1593 petition** |
| Offaly — Ciarán's imagined scriptorium | Keep the place; rebuild around evidence | **The Cross of the Scriptures, Flann Sinna, and Clonmacnoise c. 900** |
| Dublin — raid-to-market Viking composite | Keep Sihtric; replace the composite timeline | **Sihtric's first Irish silver penny, c. 997** |

Breastagh is not discarded. It becomes the first candidate for a rarer kind of
experience: a short, explicitly reconstructed **field note** about how little and how
much one damaged source can tell us.

## The test applied to every county

A story is not selected merely because it fits an existing chapter, grammar payload,
artifact, or illustration. Begin with a blank slate and test it on six questions:

1. **County claim:** would people from the county recognise this as substantially
   theirs, rather than a national story temporarily pinned there?
2. **Historical significance:** did the person, event, work, community, or object
   matter beyond local curiosity?
3. **Narrative pull:** can the true account produce a memorable opening without an
   invented protagonist doing the dramatic work?
4. **Evidence:** is there a document, object, inscription, place, recording, or
   carefully bounded tradition the learner can actually encounter?
5. **Language yield:** can 20 useful words and the next grammar step arise naturally
   from the account?
6. **Afterlife:** does the story alter how the learner sees a present-day place,
   name, phrase, institution, song, or argument?

The first story carries one additional requirement: it must be among the strongest
and most memorable stories in the entire product. Historical chronology does not
outrank the opening contract.

## 1. Mayo — Gráinne Ní Mháille

### Why she wins

Gráinne gives Mayo a famous named person, a recognisable coastline, political stakes,
family stakes, surviving correspondence, and a consequential encounter with Tudor
power. Clare Island, Kildavnet, Clew Bay, and Rockfleet make the county connection
spatial rather than nominal. The 1593 petition and state correspondence give the
story a real documentary spine.

The opening proposition is not the romanticised “pirate queen” legend. It is:

> In 1593, a maritime leader from Mayo went to Elizabeth I's court to demand action
> for herself and her family. What did she ask for, what did the state record, and
> what has later storytelling added?

This naturally earns beginner language about identity, names, origins, family,
place, sea travel, asking, and answering. The learner says who *they* are; the app
does not put invented Irish dialogue into Gráinne's mouth.

### Source starting points

- [Rockfleet Castle, Heritage Ireland](https://heritageireland.ie/unguided-sites/rockfleet-castle/)
- [The Pirate Queen of Mayo's Monuments, Heritage Ireland](https://heritageireland.ie/tag/grainne-mhaol/)
- [1593 draft concerning Gráinne and her family, National Library of Ireland](https://sources.nli.ie/Record/MS_UR_010761)

A historian must select and contextualise the primary documents. Popular claims such
as refusing to bow, communicating in Latin, a one-year marriage, or earning a name by
cutting her hair cannot enter factual narration without source-specific support.

### What happens to Breastagh

Breastagh is an excellent lesson in historical knowledge and a weak full biography.
Its damaged inscription preserves names and relationships but not a recoverable life.
The existing fictional carver, family, and practice inscription therefore do too much
of the narrative work.

Retain the strongest product assets:

- tracing Ogham from the base upward;
- carving the learner's own name;
- reading a damaged scholarly transcription;
- seeing clearly what is known, restored, disputed, and missing;
- the physical intimacy of one person's name surviving in a field.

Package them as a clearly labelled field note encountered later in the opening road.
It does not determine county completion and carries no assessed “facts” that exist
only inside the reconstruction.

## 2. Offaly — the Cross of the Scriptures at Clonmacnoise

### Why Clonmacnoise stays

Clonmacnoise is still the strongest first Offaly subject. Its position where the
Shannon met the east-west esker route made it a centre of religion, learning, trade,
craft, burial, and political power. The current scriptorium successfully generates
daily verbs, time, numbers, colours, food, and *tá*, but generic fictional scribes
carry a story that the surviving place can carry more honestly.

The rebuilt story should centre on the tenth-century Cross of the Scriptures and its
named royal patron, Flann Sinna, while Ciarán's sixth-century foundation remains the
opening layer. The proposition becomes:

> A carved cross at the crossroads of Ireland asks for a prayer for a king. Who paid
> for it, who made it, and what kind of town had grown around it?

This moves the dramatic present from an unsupported late-eighth-century gospel book
to c. 900, when the cross, political patronage, settlement, craft, and traffic can be
treated together. Excavation, graveslabs, high crosses, annals, and the surviving site
provide evidence for ordinary activity without pretending that one invented monk's
day is a recorded event.

### Source starting points

- [Clonmacnoise visitor guide, Heritage Ireland](https://heritageireland.ie/assets/uploads/2026/04/7565-OPW-Clonmacnoise-A5-visitor-guide_ENG_LR.pdf)
- [Clonmacnoise Monastic Site, Heritage Ireland](https://heritageireland.ie/visit/places-to-visit/clonmacnoise-monastic-site/)
- [High crosses and Flann Sinna's patronage, Heritage Ireland](https://heritageireland.ie/articles/high-crosses/)

The exact reading, expansion, date, and interpretation of the cross inscription need
a scholarly source packet before copy is drafted.

### What is retained and released

Retain pigments, carving, work, meals, time, the Shannon, *tá* versus *is*, and the
illuminated-initial artifact if the source story earns it. Release the race to finish
an unidentified gospel book, the implied Book of Kells connection, the Pangur Bán
continuity, and invented guides as the chapter's headline reality.

Birr's Leviathan telescope is an exceptionally strong second Offaly story about
science, engineering, observation, and Mary Rosse's photography; it should remain on
the future slate rather than displacing Clonmacnoise.

## 3. Dublin — Sihtric's penny

### Why Sihtric stays

Sihtric Silkbeard is a strong Dublin anchor, but the current chapter's 795 raid and
c. 900 market precede his actual story. Naming him in the county contract does not
make a generic two-century Viking composite his biography.

The replacement hook is concrete:

> Around 997, Dublin struck the first coins made in Ireland. The king put his name
> and his city on them: SITRIC REX DUBLIN.

One small object opens a large city: ruler, mint, market, silver, imported models,
trade routes, Irish and Norse inhabitants, and the name Dublin itself. It preserves
the excellent language fit of past tense, movement, directions, numbers, buying, and
selling while giving every activity a dated centre.

### Source starting points

- [Silver penny of SITRIC REX DUBLIN, National Museum of Ireland](https://www.museum.ie/en-IE/Collections-Research/Collection/Glendalough-Power%2C-Prayer-Pilgrimage/Artefact/Silver-penny-of-SITRIC-REX-DUBLIN/b1f12cbd-72ce-48a6-9f54-8ec41aaa42ee)
- [Vikings, Normans and medieval mints, National Museum of Ireland](https://www.museum.ie/en-IE/Collections-Research/Art-and-Industry-Collections/Art-Industry-Collections-List/Numismatics/Airgead-A-Thousand-Years-of-Irish-Coins-Currency/950-1450-Vikings%2C-Normans-and-Medieval-Mints)
- [National Museum Viking collections overview](https://www.museum.ie/en-ie/collections-research/irish-antiquities-division-collections/collections-list-%281%29/viking)

The raid of 795 becomes bounded context. It is not enacted through a fictional victim
and then leapt over to a different fictional market. Dublin's 1916, Joyce, and later
revival stories remain important future stories; they should not displace a uniquely
good beginner encounter with the city's formation and first coinage.

## The opening road

The product should not force all 32 counties into one rigid pedagogical queue. It
needs a fixed authored opening road long enough to establish the language, the
historical method, and trust. After that, the map can open into regional and thematic
routes.

| # | County story | Historical centre | Language job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Mayo — Gráinne Ní Mháille's petition | 1593 | identity, names, greetings, origin |
| 2 | Offaly — Cross of the Scriptures | c. 900 | *tá*, present activity, time, number, colour |
| 3 | Dublin — Sihtric's penny | c. 997 | past, movement, directions, trade, money |
| 4 | Meath — Hugh de Lacy and Trim | late 12th century | possession and description |
| 5 | Donegal — the Flight of the Earls | 1607 | habitual past, emotion, departure |
| 6 | Kerry — Piaras Feiritéar | 17th century | classification, opinion, comparison |
| 7 | Cork — Nano Nagle | 18th century | obligation, possibility, instruction |
| 8 | Galway — Famine letters | 1840s | family, letters, careful past consolidation |
| 9 | Roscommon — Douglas Hyde | 1893 and after | future, promises, organising |
| 10 | Armagh — border and partition | 1920s | conditional and civic language; multiple voices |
| 11 | Antrim — the Shaw's Road community | 1960s onward | contemporary community language |
| 12 | Galway — Joe Heaney | 20th century | dialect, listening, sustained speech |

Story 1 is a deliberate cold open from 1593. Story 2 visibly rewinds to c. 900, after
which the opening road moves mostly forward. The interface must make that editorial
gesture explicit; it should feel like a documentary opening and rewind, not a broken
timeline.

This is a sequencing hypothesis, not clearance for production. Stories 4–12 still
need the same clean-slate county test, source packet, sensitivity review, language
plan, and rights check before their order is fixed.

## Main stories and reconstructed field notes

The distinction is structural, not a small disclaimer.

### Main county story

- begins with a real person, object, document, place, oral record, or labelled myth;
- establishes the true account and the evidence before interpretation;
- uses real names and dated events wherever the record permits;
- gives uncertainty visible space;
- earns 20 useful words and county progress;
- may include reconstruction only after its factual foundations are understood.

### Field note

- appears infrequently between major stories;
- lasts minutes, not five sessions;
- is visibly labelled as a reconstruction, tradition, or experiment;
- can be sensorial, intimate, and imaginative;
- never hides an assessed fact inside invented dialogue;
- does not become the county's headline merely because its interaction is beautiful.

Breastagh is the prototype field note. Other candidates include a reconstructed sound,
a lost journey, a craft process, or an ordinary life known only through fragments.

## Immediate editorial consequences

1. Do not perform a mechanical county-story migration of Chapters 2 and 3. Write the
   new Offaly and Dublin source briefs first.
2. Replace the Mayo production target before commissioning public-release audio or
   final scene art. Preserve existing progress and the Ogham interaction as legacy
   content until the new migration design is known.
3. Re-evaluate Meath with the same clean-slate test before treating the four-story
   launch order as settled.
4. Add evidence type, certainty, date range, source object, and reconstruction status
   to the county-story content contract.
5. Prototype the opening road as an interface concept before rewriting three full
   JSON packs. The new product needs a broader vessel than the inherited chapter UI.
