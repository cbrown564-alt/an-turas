# Mayo historical review — Primary documentary spine, 1593

*Evidence pass against Chapters 6–8 · claims C11–C14 · name forms · 24 July 2026.*

**Scope.** Map and verify the primary spine used across the road-to-London /
in-the-record / royal-answer sequence: July 1593 petition; SP 63/170 eighteen
articles + answers; 6 September 1593 Queen→Bingham draft; related catalogue
paths; C12 court/audience boundary; L1 paraphrase audit; 1595 handoff for
Chapter 9. Checked against `content/mayo/grainne-1593-source-brief.md`,
`docs/GRAINNE-SOURCE-STRESS-TEST.md`, `docs/TNA-SP-63-170-IMAGE-REQUEST.md`,
`docs/mayo-historical-review/_WORKING-narrative-extract.md` (Ch6–9), and
public calendar / TNA / Hatfield paths listed in the bibliography.

**Method rule.** Calendar paraphrases and published images are treated as
recoverable evidence. Invented folio wording is refused. Where only the
calendar paraphrase exists publicly, that is labelled **calendar**, not
**diplomatic**.

**Overall verdict.** The folio map used in the source brief and draft
(**f. 201 questions / name-find; f. 202 answers with Burghley’s notes; f. 204
petition; Hatfield/HMC Sept draft**) is **confirmed by TNA public description
and the 1890 CSP Ireland calendar**. Claims **C11** and **C13** are
**story-ready**. **C12** is correctly split (process = story; Greenwich
embroidery = close/afterlife). **C14** is **story-ready** for unfinished
preservation, with a soft edge on exact 1595 folio numbers and outcome.
The main authoring binds are **name-form labelling** and keeping “imprisoned
kin” from swallowing Murrough.

## 3. Petition content verification

### 3.1 Recoverable calendar / published paraphrase

CSP Ireland calendars (About 1593 / July, Vol. CLXX):

1. **Petition of Grany Ne Malley of Connaught to Queen Elizabeth** — for
   **maintenance**; prays that her two sons and cousins Walter Burghke Fitz
   Theobald Reogh and Shane Burghk MacMoiler may have their lands by patent on
   surrender.
2. **Petition of Grany Ne Mally to Burghley** — to be a mean to Her Majesty to
   grant **some reasonable maintenance**; Lord Deputy to accept surrender of her
   sons (heirs of O’Flaherty and MacWilliam Bourke); desires the Queen’s letter
   for licence during her life **to invade with sword and fire all Her Majesty’s
   enemies**.

Long-circulating petition phrasing (quoted in secondary literature from the
State Papers trail; treat as recoverable English, not reinvented):

- “to grant her some reasonable maintenance for the little tyme she hath to live”
- offer of “a surrender at her hands” of sons’ / nephews’ lands
- “free libertye during her lyve to envade with sword and fire all your highness
  enemyes…”

TNA’s public caption for **f. 204** matches the maintenance petition role.

## 4. Interrogatory / answers verification

### 4.1 The eighteen questions (summary)

CSP Ireland prints all eighteen. Themes match Ch7 and the brief:

1. Parents  
2–5. First husband, sons, maintenance countries, marriages of sons  
6. Kinship of first husband to Sir Murrough then “at the court”  
7. Same for second husband and his sons  
8–10. Dower / thirds; Composition of Connacht provision for wives; Irish custom
   on widows  
11. How she has had maintenance since last husband’s death  
12–18. Kinship and territorial possession questions (Bourkes, Morisky / Murrisk,
   MacGibbon, Curramore / Mayn Connell, Achill, etc.)

The bundled **f. 201** image shows questions **1–16** on the first page;
calendar items **17–18** complete the set (consistent with a multi-page
interrogatory).

## 5. Royal draft verification (6 September 1593)

### 5.1 Who is named (HMC / BHO paraphrase)

**Present in London (suits under Bingham’s charge):**

- Sir **Morogh O’Flaharty**, Knight  
- **Grany ne Maly**  
- **Roobuck French**

**Kin for whom she sued liberty:**

- Sons **Morogh O’Flahartv** [= Murrough O’Flaherty] and **Tibbott Burk**  
- Brother **Donell O’Piper** [= Donal na Píopa]

### 5.2 What the draft orders / states

From the Cecil Papers calendar text (paraphrase level, but continuous and
specific):

1. Bingham’s opinion: their complaints have “no just cause” from him — noted,
   then overridden in part.
2. **Murrough** is **not** in trouble; he is a principal man who served the Queen
   even when his mother, with disorderly persons and “gallyes,” spoiled him;
   Bingham favours him; the Queen wishes that continued.
3. **Tibbott** justly detained for an accused letter to Brian O’Rourke’s son;
   not fully proved; he denies it; Bingham thinks he may be dismissed on bonds.
4. **Donald / Donell** imprisoned ~**7 months**, charged with company of those
   who killed soldiers in a ward; likewise dismissible on bonds.
5. Queen is **content** both be dismissed on bonds **in regard of her humble
   suit**; she is informed and departs with thanks and promises of duty, and to
   prosecute offenders.
6. Because Irish custom gives her **no title** to livelihood from her two
   husbands’ lands, Bingham is to deal with her sons **in the Queen’s name** for
   **maintenance** for her old years, and to protect them to live in peace on
   their livelihoods.
7. Favour is conditional on present dutiful showing; she has confessed former
   disorder (including evil usage of the dutiful son) and sworn to continue
   dutiful, offering to “fight in our quarrel with all the world.”

### 5.3 Bingham resistance evidence (for Ch8)

| Evidence | Strength for “did not hurry / resisted full effect” |
| --- | --- |
| Sept draft itself anticipates negotiation with sons and conditional favour | Shows paper relief ≠ automatic local outcome |
| Bingham to Burghley, **24 Nov 1593** (CSP Ireland): has **enlarged** Grany O’Mally’s son Tibbot and brother Donell Ne Pipee | Confirms eventual release **and** that weeks passed after 6 Sept |
| Bingham’s Aug 1593 framing of her “evil deeds” while conceding she should have something from sons’ livings | Hostile implementation climate |
| 1595 petitions / later SP trail (TNA blog; Chambers-line synthesis) | Strong that relief remained incomplete; she had to press again |
| Secondary consensus (Chambers, Murray, careful popular synthesis) that Bingham delayed, quartered soldiers, undermined terms | High for story-mode “resistance,” with L2 citing the paper trail |

**Do not assert on L1:** that Bingham never released anyone; that the Sept letter
alone proves a Greenwich conversation; that Murrough was among the imprisoned.

## 6. C12 audience boundary (documentary vs embroidery)

### 6.1 Documentary floor (safe for story / Ch6)

Supportable without inventing a conversation:

1. She entered the **Queen’s administrative process** in summer 1593 (petitions;
   Burghley’s interrogatory; presence in London attested in the Sept draft’s
   “being here” of Grany ne Maly with Sir Morogh and Roobuck French).
2. Burghley had already written Bingham in **July** about the three Connacht
   persons and their suits (cited inside the Sept draft).
3. Officials refer to her **being at court** (e.g. Bingham’s later reference to
   attendants upon “Grany O’Maille in her late beyinge at court” — widely cited
   from the SP trail).
4. A written royal answer followed (**6 Sept draft**).

### 6.2 Tradition / embroidery (keep out of Ch6–8 as fact)

| Motif | Placement |
| --- | --- |
| Face-to-face meeting staged at **Greenwich** with recovered dialogue | **Close / afterlife** — widely asserted in popular and some scholarly narrative; **no contemporary verbatim court transcript** of the conversation |
| Refused to bow; dagger; handkerchief; “already a queen”; Latin-only talk | **Afterlife (C16)** — Chambers and careful accounts flag as fanciful; Latin especially weak against English capability evidence |
| 1793 *Anthologia Hibernica* illustration | Reception image only |

Wikipedia’s careful line is useful editorial policy: **“Tradition but not the
written sources”** for the embroidered Greenwich court scene. The TNA blog
narrates a Greenwich encounter more affirmatively; that blog is excellent for
folio captions but **is not itself a primary transcript**.

> furnishes a meeting at Greenwich. The production story stays with what the
