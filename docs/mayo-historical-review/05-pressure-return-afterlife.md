## 2. Paired-voice guidance (Gráinne vs Bingham)

### What each voice actually says (usable contrast material)

**Gráinne (petition + 1593 answers — crafted for relief):**

- Maintenance plea: “some reasonable maintenance for the little tyme she hath to live.”
- Offer of service: leave to “envade with sword and fire” the Queen’s enemies.
- Self-framing of earlier armed livelihood: discord among chieftains “constrained” her to take arms and maintain herself and her people by land and sea.
- Bingham pressure in her voice: seizure, binding, stripping of goods; John Bingham’s force; gallows prepared for her; flight when Devil’s Hook rebelled; claim that after Perrot’s pardon she had given over her “former trade.”
- Kin stakes: fear Tibbott would be executed before just trial; brother held.

**Bingham / hostile official voice:**

- “Nurse to all rebellions in the province for this forty years” (Aug 1593 letter tradition).
- To Burghley on her London appearance: notable traitor gone over; rebel from childhood; “matter ynough of late found out against them to hang them by justice” despite pardons.
- Gendered contempt tradition: a woman who had “overstepped the part of womanhood.”
- On Tibbott (as summarised in the Queen’s draft reflecting his letters): justly detained for accused letter to O’Rourke’s son, though “it cannot be fully proved” and he denies it.
- On Donal: imprisoned ~7 months for alleged company in killing soldiers in a ward.
- On Murrough: not in trouble; dutiful; spoiled by mother’s galleys — so favoured.
- After the Queen’s favour: resistance by delay, quartering soldiers on ships/lands, undermining leave to return to sea without sureties.

### Best paired-voice practice for the app

1. **Same coast, two purposes.** Show both voices as instruments: she needs mercy and leave to live; he needs the court to refuse her and protect his presidency. Do not score them as 50/50 “both equally true.”
2. **Lead with her documentary encounter later (Ch7), but in Ch5 let hostility be audible.** Ch5’s `two-voices` page already does this correctly: conflict is revealed; accusations are not all equally proven.
3. **Quote posture, not invented dialogue.** Prefer short attributed paraphrases (“Bingham called her…”, “She told the Queen…”) over dramatised confrontation scenes.
4. **Keep institutional asymmetry visible.** Bingham had prisons, officers, and definition of lawful force; she had petition, kin, and maritime leverage under squeeze. Draft opening page already states this — keep it.
5. **Never let Bingham’s dossier open the Mayo flagship.** Stress-test rule stands: learner should meet *her* petition/answers as the handled spine; his letters are the antagonistic frame.
6. **L2 may name the famous insults; L1 need not.** “Nurse to all rebellions” and “matter enough to hang” are excellent closer-look colour; L1 can stay at “hostile official voice / disorder that demanded control.”

## 3. Owen death handling — exact L1 / L2 wording bounds

### Shared documentary floor

- Owen / Eóghan Ó Flaithbheartaigh, eldest son by Dónal an Chogaidh, dies in the mid-1580s conflict involving Captain **John Bingham**’s force during Bourke / Joyce troubles (commonly dated **1586** in secondary synthesis).
- Gráinne’s later testimony (preserved via interrogatory / petitioning materials and long quoted in secondary literature): Owen withdrew to a strong island by Bingham’s direction; entertained John Bingham’s soldiers; was bound with followers; livestock seized; followers hanged; Owen, still bound, **cruelly murdered** with many (“twelve deadly”) wounds during a midnight alarm.
- Bingham’s counter-account (Murray and careful secondary consensus): Owen, **being a prisoner, made his escape and in pursuit was slain**.

These are **competing explanations of a violent death under Bingham-side custody/force**, not a modern settled verdict.

### L1 — allowed

Use **none** of Owen’s killing as a load-bearing causal sentence on the main path, **or** at most a single non-adjudicating hinge if pedagogy absolutely requires a human cost beat:

> Bound example (only if a one-line hinge is unavoidable):  
> “Among the losses of those years was the death of her son Owen, killed in a clash involving Bingham’s officers — accounts of how he died do not agree.”

Prefer the draft’s actual choice: **omit Owen from L1 entirely** (current Ch5 main path does this).

### L1 — forbidden

- “Bingham murdered her son.”
- “Owen was treacherously murdered after offering hospitality.” (that is *her* charge — L2)
- “Owen was shot escaping.” (that is *his* charge — L2)
- Using Owen’s death as the sole or primary reason she went to London in 1593 (Tibbott/Donal 1593 detentions are the tipping stake).

### L2 / closer look — allowed (matches current `owen-boundary` page)

Current draft body is within bounds:

> “Gráinne blamed Bingham’s side for the death of her son Owen; the surviving accounts conflict over the circumstances. That accusation belongs in closer view because it deepens the stakes, but the main story does not pronounce a settled murder where the record does not support one.”

**Recommended slight sharpening (optional):**

> “In her later testimony she said Owen offered hospitality to Bingham’s officers, was bound, and was murdered while still a prisoner. Bingham’s side said he was a prisoner who escaped and was slain in pursuit. The death is real; the settled ‘murder’ verdict is not.”

Detail (twelve wounds, island hospitality, hanged followers) may appear in L2/L3 with attribution to **her account**, not as neutral narration.

### Board rule (reaffirm)

**C09 stays closer-look only.** Do not promote Owen’s killing onto Ch5’s authored exit or the voyage-chart broken-path mark as a labelled “murder.”
