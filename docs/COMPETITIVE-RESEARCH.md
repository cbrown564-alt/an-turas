# Competitive Research: Irish Language Learning Landscape

*Prepared for An Turas. Focus: pedagogical/andragogical style, features,
positioning, and failed experiments. Updated 2026-07-05.*

## Executive summary

The Irish learning market is crowded at the **habit** and **vocabulary** layers,
contested at the **rigour** layer, and **empty at the narrative–identity layer**
An Turas targets. Competitors fall into six pedagogical families; none combine
historical narrative, visual world-building, explicit grammar for anglophones,
and adult-oriented (andragogical) respect for prior school-Irish experience.

Three strategic reads:

1. **blas.** owns "serious Celtic grammar" — FSRS, mutation drilling, TEG
   alignment — and is the highest direct product threat.
2. **Duolingo** owns default mindshare but is actively **degrading** the Irish
   product (AI audio, contributor exit, implicit grammar), creating a large
   drop-out funnel.
3. **Nobody owns "learn Irish by falling in love with the story of Ireland."**
   Closest attempts (Bitesize, Gaeilgeoir, DCU MOOCs) treat culture as
   seasoning, not spine.

The failed experiments section matters: Irish tech repeatedly hits the same
walls — **dialect/audio fidelity**, **mutation pedagogy**, **retention without
manipulative gamification**, and **scale vs. cultural legitimacy**.

## Competitor profiles

### 1. Duolingo Irish — gamified implicit immersion

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pedagogy** | Implicit "immersion": pattern exposure without systematic grammar explanation. Behaviorist habit loop (streaks, XP, leagues, hearts). Andragogically weak for Irish: treats it like Spanish-with-weird-spelling. |
| **Andragogy** | Assumes learner-as-game-player, not identity-motivated adult. No respect for latent school-Irish knowledge; no "why school never explained mutations." |
| **Features** | Full course free; SRS-like review; some stories/audio lessons; speech exercises with unreliable feedback. |
| **Positioning** | "I'm learning Irish" ≈ Duolingo for millions. Distribution moat; depth deficit. |
| **Threat to An Turas** | High distribution, low product depth. **Drop-outs are the funnel.** |

**Documented weaknesses:** No mutation system teaching; AI-generated audio replacing
native recordings (2022–23); dialect inconsistency; `cuid` and other grammar errors;
A2 plateau; no graded reading or real conversation. Community contributors (Team
Gaeilge) exited August 2024; course future uncertain.

---

### 2. blas. — explicit Celtic rigour (highest product threat)

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pedagogy** | **Explicit instruction + FSRS-5.** Grammar lessons → targeted drills. Mutations as first-class skill (recognition, cued, free-form, contextual). Vocabulary in grammatical context. Andragogical: "no mascots, no confetti, no streak guilt." |
| **Andragogy** | Built for **adults returning to Irish/Welsh** after school. Placement test skips known material. Mastery grids, leech detection, weekly reports — adult need for measurable progress. |
| **Features** | 12+ exercise types; graded reading A1–C1; text conversation tutor; writing + Leaving Cert/TEG/GCSE prep; speech recognition; daily "real world" links (podcasts, radio); 3 dialects; free browser grammar checker; iOS/Android/web. |
| **Positioning** | "The app built for Irish, not translated from Spanish." Owns **rigour**. |
| **Gap vs. An Turas** | Culture/history appears in reading passages, not as retention engine. No narrative journey, artifacts, or identity framing. |

---

### 3. Say Something in Irish (SSi) — audio-first confidence

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pedagogy** | **Anti-grammar by design.** Listen → pause → speak. Teaches "edges" between words; mistakes as method. Strong andragogical honesty: "five minutes daily beats Sunday cramming." |
| **Andragogy** | Targets adults who've internalised "I'm bad at languages." Confidence-first; no reading/writing burden. |
| **Features** | Audio-only sessions; active forum (Welsh-strong); Irish course less mature than Welsh. |
| **Positioning** | "You've got a streak, but you can't speak." Anti-Duolingo speaking pitch. |
| **Gap vs. An Turas** | No literacy, no mutations, no culture-as-spine. Irish content required methodology compromises (e.g. unnatural `labhraím` patterns) per their own forum disclosures. |

---

### 4. Bitesize Irish — diaspora community + Gaeilge Gach Lá

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pedagogy** | **Gaeilge Gach Lá** (Irish Every Day): identity-integrated habit, not perfectionism. Small steps, seasonal/folklore depth, community prompts. Communicative + reflective rather than drill-heavy. |
| **Andragogy** | Strong adult framing: "reconnect," "reclamation," "webs of meaning." 15 years of email list trust. Tiers: Leabharlann (self-paced) → Tinteán (daily prompts) → Ciorcal Beo (live). |
| **Features** | ~150 bite lessons; podcast (ended 2016, strategic pivot); newsletter 10k+; native-speaker audio; Facebook community. |
| **Positioning** | Owns **diaspora email/community** and gentle cultural depth. |
| **Gap vs. An Turas** | Culture enriches method; doesn't **gate** progress through historical narrative. Visual identity modest; school-Irish re-learners not explicitly served with grammar repair. |

---

### 5. Gaeilgeoir AI — AI conversation + heritage marketing

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pedagogy** | AI tutor conversations; 1000-word immersion; Leaving Cert oral prep; gamified quizzes. Speak-first with instant feedback. |
| **Andragogy** | Targets diaspora heritage reconnect + exam students. "Native speaker in your pocket" — convenience over cultural authority. |
| **Features** | Scenario dialogues; pronunciation support; word saving; €9.99/mo; Android Dec 2025 (~100+ downloads). |
| **Positioning** | "Real Irish for real life" + heritage/culture in marketing copy. |
| **Gap vs. An Turas** | Culture is **marketing layer**, not authored narrative world. No historian/pedagogue review signal. Scale unproven. |

---

### 6. Let's Learn Irish — live community (3Cs model)

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pedagogy** | University of Galway **3Cs**: Connection, Communication, Collaboration. Sociolinguistic, task-based, live Comhrá sessions. Explicitly anti-"app-only" for fluency. |
| **Andragogy** | Adult learners as community members, not app users. CEFR A0–B2 with multiple teachers/dialects. |
| **Features** | Live courses, weekly Comhrá (Zoom), workshops, video library, member discounts. |
| **Positioning** | "Global Gaeltacht" — **community as retention engine**. |
| **Gap vs. An Turas** | Not scalable self-paced narrative; potential **partner** (graduate pathway) rather than competitor. |

---

### 7. Gaelchultúr / Ranganna.ie — institutional communicative standard

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pedagogy** | Communicative approach; graded QQI-aligned syllabuses; instant feedback exercises; audio/video. Pre-communicative → communicative → post-communicative phases. |
| **Andragogy** | Professional/adult learner focus: public sector certs, Leaving Cert, Hibernia, EPV teacher CPD. |
| **Features** | Self-paced e-learning + live classes; European Language Label (2009); recognised NFQ levels. |
| **Positioning** | "Best place to learn Irish online" — **institutional credibility**. |
| **Gap vs. An Turas** | Functional everyday Irish, not identity/history journey. Free baseline sets price sensitivity. |

---

### 8. All About Irish (Patricia Ní Chasaide) — Structured Irish

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pedagogy** | **Structured Irish**: early sentence architecture, not rote phrases. Michel Thomas lineage; songs/poems for rhythm; Connacht audio. Live Zoom + self-study hybrid. |
| **Andragogy** | Explicitly addresses Duolingo refugees: "confusion from other methods." TEG A2/B1 courses. |
| **Features** | Video + conversation audio + worksheets; lifetime access; dedicated app. |
| **Positioning** | Premium small-cohort teaching; west-of-Ireland authenticity. |
| **Gap vs. An Turas** | Structure without **historical world**; instructor-led scale ceiling. |

---

### 9. Michel Thomas / Pimsleur / Mango — generalist audio/conversation

| Product | Pedagogy | Irish-specific notes |
|---------|----------|---------------------|
| **Michel Thomas** | Teacher-led audio; "no memorising"; building-block sentences | Patricia Mac Eoin/Eilís Ní Dhúill; good grammar intuition, weak mutations/vocab breadth |
| **Pimsleur** | 30-min audio; graduated interval recall; **Munster dialect only** | Commute-friendly; no reading/mutations |
| **Mango** | Short visual lessons; culture notes; voice comparison | One of 70+ languages; shallow Irish depth |

**Positioning:** Proven methods, **not Irish-native products**. Useful supplements, not
identity destinations.

---

### 10. Drops / Memrise / Anki — vocabulary layer

| Product | Pedagogy | Positioning |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| **Drops** | 5-min/day visual vocab games; SRS; Irish culture topics (GAA, trad music) as **word lists** | Honest scope: vocab only, no grammar |
| **Memrise** | Community SRS decks; **no official Irish course**; community content phased out | Quality variance; dying as primary path |
| **Anki** | DIY FSRS | Power users only |

---

### 11. DCU Fáilte ar Líne — institutional MOOC

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Pedagogy** | **Languaging** (action-oriented, CHAT-derived); culture + language for diaspora; emotion-aware design (Pekrun CVT research). |
| **Andragogy** | Adult MOOC learners across 140 countries; 45k+ registered on Irish 101 pathway. |
| **Features** | Free FutureLearn courses Irish 101–108; pathway to online degree. |
| **Positioning** | Authoritative, free, diaspora-serving. |
| **Gap vs. An Turas** | Academic MOOC UX; not daily narrative pull or mobile-native craft. |

## Failed experiments and cautionary tales

Products, pivots, and industry moves that **failed, stalled, or taught expensive
lessons** — directly relevant to An Turas bets on narrative retention, audio, and
cultural legitimacy.

### A. Duolingo: the AI-first regression (Irish + Welsh parallel)

**What happened:** Native-speaker Irish audio replaced with AI TTS (2022–23).
Community course team disbanded (Aug 2024). Welsh course **mothballed** Nov 2023
— frozen with errors introduced in a final update; 675k active Welsh learners
stranded.

**Pedagogical failure:** For Irish, broad/slender consonant distinction is
load-bearing. AI voices trained on non-native or mixed data **teach wrong
phonology at scale**. Immersion without explanation **plus** bad audio =
fossilised error.

**Andragogical failure:** Streaks sustain **app opens**, not **identity growth**.
SSi's founder quote captures it: "You've got a streak, but you can't speak."

**Lesson for An Turas:** Never ship audio without native-speaker review. Duolingo
drop-outs are your funnel — but only if you're visibly the **anti-slop** option.

---

### B. Gamification-as-retention (industry-wide)

**Experiment:** Replace intrinsic motivation (heritage, story, community) with
extrinsic loops (XP, leagues, guilt streaks).

**Outcome for Irish:** Massive top-of-funnel (1M+ Duolingo Irish learners
historically), **brutal mid-funnel drop-off** at mutations/copula. Learners report
shame about broken streaks, not shame about not speaking Irish — wrong emotion for
this language.

**Lesson for An Turas:** The thesis ("connection not streaks") is differentiated
but **unproven**. Competitors prove gamification works for **opens**; none prove
narrative works for **daily return** yet. Hybrid risk: you may need gentle rituals
(recarve, amárach hooks) without Duolingo-style manipulation.

---

### C. Culture-as-marketing without editorial depth (Gaeilgeoir and peers)

**Experiment:** Heritage Instagram copy + AI chat + "immersive stories" in feature
lists.

**Outcome:** Indistinguishable from dozens of AI language wrappers; tiny traction
(100+ Android downloads at launch). Leaving Cert oral prep is concrete; "cultural
journey" is not evidenced in product depth.

**Lesson for An Turas:** Culture moat requires **historian + pedagogue sign-off
per chapter**, not feature bullet points. One viral "they got the tuiseal ginideach
wrong" thread destroys positioning.

---

### D. Narrative + visual identity without rigour (Chineasy parallel)

**Experiment (adjacent market):** Chineasy built beautiful visual etymology + culture
podcasts + SRS — closest analogue to "story + characters + literacy."

**Failure mode:** Users report **introducing characters without testing**, game
progression without weakness diagnosis, subscription fatigue. Culture content split
across multiple apps (Chineasy vs Talk Chineasy) — narrative and conversation
divorced.

**Lesson for An Turas:** Visual narrative must stay **coupled to retrieval**
(recarve, exercises, listen/echo). Illustration without spaced recall becomes
entertainment, not learning.

---

### E. Memrise community courses phased out

**Experiment:** Crowdsourced Irish decks with native video.

**Outcome:** Platform pivoted away from community content (2024); **no official
Irish course**. Ecosystem fragmenting.

**Lesson:** Don't depend on third-party SRS ecosystems for core curriculum. Own
content-as-data (the JSON pipeline).

---

### F. An Scéalaí / Irish WordBricks / Tapadóir (academic–commercial gap)

| Project | Intent | Failure/stagnation mode |
|---------|--------|-------------------------|
| **An Scéalaí** (TCD/ABAIR iCALL) | Speech-aware tutoring, teacher-in-the-loop | Still "work in progress"; classroom adoption slow; ASR for Irish immature |
| **Irish WordBricks** | NLP-powered sentence building for schools | Curriculum-aligned but **school-only**; didn't become consumer retention product |
| **Tapadóir** | EN→GA machine translation for government | Pilot completed; MT quality below "editable" threshold for professionals |

**Lesson:** Irish tech wins in **partnership with educators**, loses as
**standalone consumer apps** without distribution and cultural authority. ABAIR
licensing is a gating experiment for An Turas audio strategy.

---

### G. SSi Irish: methodology vs. language reality

**Experiment:** Import Welsh SSi engine to Irish with minimal grammar.

**Outcome:** Forum admissions of **"acceptable enough"** compromises on
Irish-specific patterns; TTS voices until native recording affordable; Irish course
less mature than Welsh.

**Lesson:** Celtic structural similarity ≠ pedagogy portability. Irish mutations and
copula need explicit passes SSi deliberately avoids.

---

### H. Pronunciation scoring / speech recognition

**Experiment:** Multiple apps promise "speak with confidence" (Duolingo, Mango,
Gaeilgeoir, blas. speech modes).

**Outcome for Irish:** ASR for Irish remains research-grade; learners report apps
accepting nonsense audio. An Turas correctly punted v1 to **echo (ungraded)** per U8.

**Lesson:** Listening-first + human-reviewed TTS beats fake pronunciation scores.

---

### I. Institutional free baseline (DCU, Ranganna)

**Not failures** — but **positioning constraints:** high-quality free MOOCs and
Ranganna courses anchor willingness-to-pay at zero for "structured Irish." Paid apps
must sell **what institutions don't**: narrative pull, craft, identity arc, offline
artifact collection.

---

### J. Bitesize podcast end (2016)

**Experiment:** Podcast as community top-of-funnel.

**Outcome:** Deliberate sunset to focus on membership tiers — not a product failure,
a **channel experiment concluded**.

**Lesson:** Content companies must kill channels that don't convert to retention
mechanics you control.

## Implications for An Turas

### Where the white space is real

**"Learn Irish by falling in love with the story of Ireland"** remains unoccupied.
Competitors own:

- **Habit** → Duolingo
- **Rigour** → blas.
- **Diaspora list** → Bitesize
- **Live community** → Let's Learn Irish
- **Institutional credibility** → Gaelchultúr / DCU

None combine: **authored historical spine + explain-then-drill Irish pedagogy +
visual artifact retention + TEG ladder + adult re-learner respect.**

### Andragogical requirements (from competitor gaps)

Adult Irish learners consistently reject:

- Being treated as blank slates (school-Irish re-learners)
- Childish mascots and streak guilt
- Grammar mystery (Duolingo) **or** grammar dryness without meaning (pure drill)

They respond to:

- **Why** explanations (blas., All About Irish, Structured Irish)
- **Identity** hooks (Bitesize, diaspora marketing)
- **Progress they can trust** (TEG, not XP)
- **Community belonging** (LLI, Bitesize Ciorcal) — potential Phase 4 partnership,
  not Phase 1 competition

An Turas's "three registers" (scene / note / exercise) and recarve mechanic are
pedagogy innovations **no competitor has shipped** — but they're unvalidated until
playtest answers: *did anything pull you back for session two?*

### Competitive responses to monitor

| Signal | Risk |
|--------|------|
| blas. adds "culture daily" links | Encroaches identity adjacency without narrative cost |
| Gaeilgeoir adds "history modules" | Shallow copy of positioning |
| Duolingo Irish AI stories at scale | Floods narrative channel with slop; raises quality bar |
| SSi + Foras/Conradh partnership | Could ship speaking-first Irish with institutional backing |

### Failed experiments to internalise

1. **Bad audio at scale** is worse than silence (Duolingo TTS).
2. **Gamification** opens apps; it doesn't teach Irish (mutations prove it).
3. **Culture marketing** without editorial board is fragile (Gaeilgeoir).
4. **Narrative without retrieval** doesn't stick (Chineasy complaints).
5. **Platform dependency** (Memrise, Duolingo contributor model) evaporates.
6. **Pronunciation theatre** erodes trust; echo-ungraded is honest.
7. **Welsh mothball** is the canary for Irish: if Duolingo deprioritises Irish, your
   funnel grows — but only if you're visibly the serious destination.
