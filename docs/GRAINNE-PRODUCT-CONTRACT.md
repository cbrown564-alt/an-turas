# Gráinne / Mayo product contract

*Decisions from the 11 July 2026 product grilling. This sharpens D12–D15 and is the
input to source research and low-fidelity storyboarding; it is not yet a finding that
Gráinne can carry Mayo alone.*

## County journey

- The learner completes one county at a time.
- A county contains a designated core story arc made of four to six episodes, each
  roughly 8–12 minutes.
- All core encounters complete → county turns gold → return to the island → one
  designated next county turns green.
- The historical and language spine chooses the next county. The island is visible
  but is not an open county picker.
- Episodes are immediately available. The arc is bingeable, with satisfying authored
  exits and returns; there are no artificial timers.

## Flagship and side-story rule

Start by testing whether one flagship story can carry the entire county arc. It must
provide both enough sourced human and historical change for a satisfying episodic
narrative and enough authentic dramatic ground to introduce, use, and revisit 20
useful words.

If it fails either test, add side stories to fill the demonstrated gap. Side stories
are permitted remedies, not planned filler or a variety quota. Apply this rule to
every county.

## Mayo story hypothesis

Test Gráinne's broader life as Mayo's sole flagship, with the 1593 crisis, journey to
London, documentary exchange, and negotiation as the organising centre. Do not make a
1593-only document lesson or an exhaustive cradle-to-grave biography.

The dramatic spine is:

> Can Gráinne preserve her family, authority, and way of life as Tudor power closes
> around Clew Bay?

An episode belongs only if it changes the learner's answer. “Way of life” must mean a
specific Gaelic maritime, kinship, economic, and political order. The story must not
romanticise it or omit Gráinne's raiding, coercion, wealth, and pragmatic alliances.

## Episode contract

Every core episode must contain at least one meaningful historical discovery that
changes understanding and one meaningful use of Irish tied to the dramatic stakes.
The rest may ask only for attention. Decorative tapping, immediate checks of the
previous sentence, and entirely observational core episodes do not satisfy the floor.

## Twenty-word contract

- The public promise means exactly 20 distinct lexical headwords per county.
- Tokens, multiword expressions, constructions, inflections, and emphatic variants
  cannot inflate the count.
- Teach headwords inside useful phrases; phrases are vehicles, not quota units.
- Every word must appear at a dramatic or practical need, be used meaningfully by the
  learner, and return in a later encounter or episode, preferably in a changed
  context.
- These authored opportunities are required; demonstrated mastery is not required to
  turn the county gold.

Irish is first encountered through sound, meaning, and dramatic use. Explain grammar
briefly only when it enables the next action or clarifies a contrast already noticed.
Deeper grammar is optional, no grammar note gates progress, and the TEG-aligned spine
still controls sequencing behind the story.

## Evidence contract

Every source carrying a material historical claim requires a story view integrated
when the source changes the narrative; an optional closer look with transcription,
translation, context, and bounded uncertainty; and a full editorial record with
citations, rights state, claim ledger, scholarly notes, and review history. The story
must work using only the first depth. Progressive disclosure cannot conceal weak
sourcing.

## Collection and completion

- Evidence the learner handles accumulates quietly in Mayo's county record.
- Earned Irish accumulates in the learner's usable language.
- These are not presented as equal collectible systems.
- Completing Mayo awards one personal artifact: a learner-authored voyage chart
  tracing Clew Bay–London–Mayo, carrying Gráinne's name, the learner's name and Irish
  self-identification, and restrained marks tied to handled evidence.
- The chart is a record of the learner's journey, not a replica historical document.

## Explicit non-goals

- forcing all 20 words into one episode or documentary encounter;
- adding side stories before the flagship has failed a stated test;
- exhaustive biography or a catalogue of famous incidents;
- pretending the learner causes or witnesses history;
- decorative interaction or constant comprehension checks;
- compulsory grammar notes;
- dossier-first navigation, equal collectible currencies, or an open county picker;
- artificial waiting between episodes;
- fabricated historical artifacts.

## Questions still to answer

### Story and sources

- Which four to six sourced changes in Gráinne's broader life best answer the dramatic
  question and create satisfying episode boundaries?
- Does that storyboard pass both flagship tests, or does Mayo need a side story?
- Which 1593 documents form the learner-facing source sequence, and which claims about
  earlier and later life are strong enough for the story view?
- How do later stories about bowing, Latin, hair-cutting, marriage, and “pirate queen”
  enter as afterlife without becoming either fact or warning labels?

### Language

- Which exact 20 Connacht-oriented headwords belong to Mayo?
- Where are the dramatic need, learner use, and later reuse for each word?
- Which constructions and optional grammar explanations make those words useful
  speech rather than a thematic list?

### Episode and return design

- What discovery and Irish action satisfy the binding floor in each episode?
- What local question, exit, next hook, and authored return belong to each episode?
- On app resume, when should the learner re-enter the current episode directly and
  when should they see the island first?

### Completion and repeatability

- How is the voyage chart assembled progressively without exposing a reward system
  ahead of the story?
- What parts of the episode and artifact grammar are reusable across counties without
  making every story feel templated?
- Which minimum source, rights, historian, Irish-language, audio, and visual package
  must pass before storyboard approval and before implementation?
- How do optional later additions to a completed county behave without silently
  changing its core arc or revoking gold?
