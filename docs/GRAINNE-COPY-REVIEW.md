# Gráinne full-prototype copy review

*13–14 July 2026 · editorial review of the six-episode Mayo prototype in
`GrainneFullStory.swift`. The replacement voice and screen-by-screen pass were signed
off for implementation on 14 July after the reported historian and Irish-language
pedagogue review. Native-speaker audio QA and the moderated complete-arc test remain
separate gates.*

## Verdict

The prototype has the right historical spine and almost none of the felt life that
should make that spine compelling. Too much of the current copy sounds like a product
team explaining its information architecture: *system, structure, machinery,
administrative form, documentary act, registers*. Those ideas matter in the source
guide. In the story they arrive before sea, stone, weather, family and danger have had
time to become real.

The result is accurate but airless. Screens often restate the storyboard's purpose
instead of telling the next part of the story. Irish then appears as a labelled bundle
at the end of an episode, so it feels like payment demanded after reading rather than
another way to see what just happened.

The rewrite should keep the evidence discipline and change the centre of gravity:

- concrete thing before abstract interpretation;
- consequence before curriculum explanation;
- one live question per episode, not one rhetorical question per screen;
- Irish at the moment an English word stops being enough;
- short source boundaries in the story, with method and taxonomy in the source guide;
- no copy about what “the learner” is doing inside the learner-facing story;
- no claim that Gráinne thought or said words the record does not preserve.

## Voice target

Warm, close and exact. The narrator knows this coast and knows the limits of the
record. Sentences may be lyrical when the image is specific; uncertainty should be
plain rather than defensive. Prefer verbs that move: *held, crossed, named, asked,
ordered, refused, returned*. Use institutional nouns only when the institution itself
enters the action.

The desired rhythm is:

> The tide reaches the foot of Rockfleet. A boat can leave from the door. A family
> can hold a coast from here—and lose it from here too.

That voice is more alive than “Rockfleet makes authority spatial,” without making a
larger historical claim.

## Global interface copy

## Screen-by-screen replacement pass

The following is the signed-off story read. Where the later source review fixed a more
precise object or rendering—most notably the July `SP 63/170` interrogatory in Episode
4—the source decision takes precedence over the earlier draft wording below.

### Episode 1 — An Bá / Clew Bay

#### 1.1 Opening

- **Title:** `Where the road is water`
- **Question:** `How do you hold a coast made of islands?`
- **Body:** `Clew Bay breaks the Mayo shore into water, islands and narrow landings. Here, a boat can join places that a road cannot.`
- **Detail:** `To know this bay is to know where people, news and force can move—and where weather can stop them.`
- **Next:** `Follow the water`

#### 1.2 Recognition and danger

- **Title:** `They knew her as a captain`
- **Body:** `English officials wrote of Gráinne as a woman who led at sea. Their words are hostile, but the hostility gives something away: they took her power seriously.`
- **Detail:** `“Pirate queen” is the later legend. The older record shows boats, followers, wealth and force on a contested coast.`
- **Source label:** `An English official describes Gráinne at sea`
- **Next:** `Hear the bay in Irish`

#### 1.3 Language

- **Title:** `Farraige. Bá. Long. Áit.`
- **Lead:** `Sea. Bay. Ship. Place. Four words draw the world beneath her story.`
- **Prompt:** `Listen first. Then match each sound to the coast in front of you.`
- **Origin line:** Introduce *as* through one complete recorded line, not as a fifth loose
  chip: *Is as Maigh Eo mé.*
- **Next:** `Enter Rockfleet`

### Episode 2 — Carraig a Chabhlaigh / Rockfleet

#### 2.1 The held place

- **Title:** `A castle at the tide line`
- **Question:** `What can be held from here?`
- **Body:** `At Rockfleet, the tide comes close to the walls. Boats extend the castle across the bay; the castle gives those boats a defended home.`
- **Detail:** `This is not a lonely ruin in Gráinne's story. It is harbour, household and stronghold together.`
- **Next:** `Meet the household`

#### 2.2 The names at stake

- **Title:** `Her power had family names`
- **Body:** `Children, brother, followers, land and boats appear together in the crisis of 1593. Harm one part and the whole household feels it.`
- **Detail:** `The state papers do not give us a private family portrait. They show why these people mattered politically.`
- **Source label:** `The 1593 papers name the family in the case`
- **Next:** `Name what is held`

#### 2.3 Language

- **Title:** `Caisleán. Teaghlach. Mac. Bean.`
- **Lead:** `The castle is stone. The household is people. The record will put both under pressure.`
- **Prompt:** `Hear each word, then find the person or place it names.`
- **Next:** `See what closes in`

### Episode 3 — An Brú / The pressure

#### 3.1 The break

- **Title:** `Her sons are held. Her living is cut away.`
- **Question:** `What road remains when power closes the others?`
- **Body:** `Richard Bingham's government bears down on Gráinne's family and livelihood. Tibbott and Donal are held. The boats and maintenance that sustain her authority are at risk.`
- **Detail:** `This is the turn in the story: staying on the coast will not free them.`
- **Next:** `Read the two accounts`

#### 3.2 Competing purposes

- **Title:** `The same coast, told two ways`
- **Body:** `Bingham writes as a governor defending severity. Gráinne petitions as a leader seeking release and maintenance. Each account wants the state to act.`
- **Detail:** `Purpose does not make either source useless. It tells us how to read what each one can prove.`
- **Interaction labels:** `Bingham needs to justify` / `Gráinne needs to obtain`
- **Next:** `Choose the road that remains`

#### 3.3 Language

- **Title:** `Caill. Deartháir. Iarr. Téigh.`
- **Lead:** `Lose. Brother. Ask. Go. The words now form a chain: loss makes the asking necessary; asking means going.`
- **Prompt:** `Listen for the action that turns the story toward London.`
- **Next:** `Cross to London`

### Episode 4 — An Litir / Crossing and record

#### 4.1 Crossing

- **Title:** `The sea road ends in rooms of paper`
- **Question:** `How does a Mayo leader make the English state hear her?`
- **Body:** `Gráinne crosses to London with a case to press. There, claims become petitions, questions, answers and draft instructions.`
- **Detail:** `No surviving source gives us her conversation with Elizabeth. The papers show the case moving through government.`
- **Next:** `Open the September draft`

#### 4.2 Name-find

- **Title:** `There she is: “Grany ne Maly”`
- **Body:** `An English clerk bends her name into an administrative spelling. The form is strange; the person is not lost. Her family appears beside her.`
- **Detail:** `Finding the name is not a puzzle pasted onto the story. It is the moment the voyage becomes visible in the record.`
- **Prompt:** `Find the name the clerk was trying to hold.`
- **Next:** `Hear her name restored`

#### 4.3 Identity

- **Title:** `Ainm. Mise. Tar.`
- **Lead:** `The paper calls her “Grany ne Maly.” Hear the name she carries in Irish: Gráinne Ní Mháille.`
- **Teaching sequence:** Hear *Is mise Gráinne.* Then change only the name. Hear *Is as Maigh Eo mé.* Then change only the place.
- **Instruction:** `Keep the pattern. Make the lines yours.`
- **Completion:** `Your name has entered Irish; it has not entered Gráinne's story.`
- **Next:** `See what answer returns`

### Episode 5 — An Freagra / The answer

#### 5.1 The order

- **Title:** `The answer becomes an order`
- **Question:** `Can paper change what happens on the Mayo coast?`
- **Body:** `Instructions leave the court for Bingham. They order release and relief in response to the case Gráinne has brought.`
- **Detail:** `The state has answered. That is not the same as saying the order will be obeyed.`
- **Source label:** `Draft instructions to Bingham, September 1593`
- **Next:** `Follow the paper home`

#### 5.2 The withheld effect

- **Title:** `The order travels. Relief does not fully follow.`
- **Body:** `Bingham does not give the instruction full effect. Gráinne has forced an answer from the centre of power, but the pressure on the coast is not over.`
- **Interaction:** Ask the learner to distinguish two propositions: `The government ordered relief` and `The order ended the conflict`. The second is the one the evidence cannot support.
- **Next:** `Name what remains unfinished`

#### 5.3 Language

- **Title:** `Freagair. Tabhair. Arís.`
- **Lead:** `Answer. Give. Again. One verb names what the court did; another names what was withheld; the last opens the road back.`
- **Prompt:** `Listen, then place each action on the right side of the order.`
- **Next:** `Return to the coast`

### Episode 6 — Ar Ais / Return and coast

#### 6.1 The open return

- **Title:** `The line home does not close`
- **Question:** `What remains when the royal answer is not enough?`
- **Body:** `Further pressure brings further petitions. Gráinne's journey to London has changed the record, but it has not made the Mayo coast safe or settled.`
- **Detail:** `The surviving trail asks us to hold achievement and incompletion together.`
- **Next:** `Stand on the shore today`

#### 6.2 Place and afterlife

- **Title:** `The coast remembers in different ways`
- **Body:** `Rockfleet still stands beside Clew Bay. The state papers preserve a journey and a demand. Later stories preserve an image of defiance.`
- **Detail:** `Place, document and legend can all matter without being treated as the same kind of evidence.`
- **Language prompt:** `Hear cósta—coast—then find the earlier words that still belong here: bá, áit, caisleán.`
- **Next:** `Complete the return line`

#### 6.3 Completion

- **Title:** `Back to Mayo, carrying an answer`
- **Question:** `What will make this story return to you?`
- **Body:** `The route now joins Clew Bay, London and Mayo again. Along it sit twenty Irish words for sea, family, loss, asking, answer and return.`
- **Detail:** `The chart records a documented crossing. Your two Irish lines sit beside it as something you made here.`
- **Action:** `Draw the final line home`
- **Exit:** `Carry Mayo with you`

## Connection edits outside the 18 beats

The dossier should promise the experience in human terms. Replace “state paper ·
person · sea route” with `A Mayo leader · a family in danger · a case carried to
London`. Keep the document date nearby, but do not make metadata the invitation.

The person page and Episode 1 currently repeat “maritime leader,” “Mayo coast” and
“documentary record.” Let the person page establish identity and stakes; let Episode 1
establish geography. Repetition should deepen a word in a changed context, not repeat
the same summary.

The source guide may retain precise terms such as *administrative record*, *draft
instruction*, *supported interpretation* and *afterlife*. The story should link to
that precision without speaking in its register all the time.

## Editorial acceptance checklist

**Approval state:** the story and interface copy is approved and implemented. The
historical claims, 20-word weave, complete spoken lines, guides and distractors have
the reported first-pass specialist approval recorded in `STATUS.md`. The final two
learner-memory checks belong to the moderated complete-arc round. Generated clips
remain blocked from release sign-off until native-speaker and editorial listening QA.

- Read all 18 beats aloud as one sitting; remove any sentence that merely explains the
  job of the previous sentence.
- Verify every sentence involving Sidney, Bingham, imprisonment, maintenance, the
  September draft, royal relief and later petitions against the claim ledger.
- Have the Irish pedagogue approve complete spoken lines, pronunciation guides and the
  semantic distractors used in recall.
- Test whether a learner can retell the causal chain without prompts: coast → household
  → pressure → crossing → answer → incomplete return.
- Test whether the learner remembers at least one Irish word because of the story
  moment that needed it.
- Do not ship generated clips merely because they exist in the bundle; retain the
  native-speaker and editorial listening gate in the audio manifest.
