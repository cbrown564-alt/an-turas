## Dramatic question

> Can Gráinne preserve her family, authority, and way of life as Tudor power closes
> around Clew Bay?

“Way of life” means the Gaelic maritime, kinship, economic, and political order of
Umhaill / Clew Bay: fleet, shore castles, levies and raids, clientship, marriage
alliances, and maintenance by land and sea. The arc must show raiding, coercion,
wealth, and pragmatic alliances as well as Crown pressure.

## Source landscape (what actually survives)

Gráinne is absent from the Irish annals. The usable documentary spine is English
administrative: petitions, Burghley's eighteen articles of interrogatory and her
answers, Bingham's hostile letters, the Queen's September 1593 instructions, and
later 1595 petitions. Place evidence (Rockfleet / Carraig a Chabhlaigh, Clare Island,
Clew Bay) carries county claim. There is no contemporary portrait.

Primary catalogue anchors for the brief:

## Claims strong enough for story view

Use these without apologetic framing; closer look carries uncertainty:

1. She maintained herself and followers by land and sea from Clew Bay / Umhaill.
2. English officials recognised her as a sea captain with galleys and fighting men.
3. Rockfleet (Carraig a Chabhlaigh) is a surviving Mayo place tied to her second
   marriage household and later residence.
4. By 1593 Bingham's pressure, kin imprisonment, and loss of livelihood pushed her to
   petition Elizabeth and travel to court.
5. Burghley's articles and her answers are the closest surviving self-account.
6. The September 1593 royal draft names her sons and brother and orders relief /
   investigation over Bingham's objections.
7. The victory was incomplete: she had to press again in the mid-1590s.

## English-source bias (must not hide)

The documentary voice is the Tudor state's. Story view can still centre Gráinne if
the learner finds *her* petition and answers, not Bingham's dossier first. Closer look
must say: Irish annals are silent; we hear her through English paperwork and hostile
officials; she is a skilled petitioner shaping that record.
