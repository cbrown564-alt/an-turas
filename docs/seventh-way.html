An Seisear — the six families

Every Irish app is a bet on why you'll come back.

The market sorts into six pedagogical families, and each one is really a theory of retention: streaks, mastery, the sound of your own voice, a community that knows your name, a tutor on demand, or a certificate at the end. Below, each family's core loop is rebuilt as a working specimen — tap them, they answer back.

These are reconstructions from research, not screenshots. Each compresses a real product's defining moment into one interactive screen, styled the way that product actually feels — because the feel is the strategy. Notice what each one is optimised to make you do, and what it quietly gives up to do it.

An Léarscáil — the territory

The field everyone can see, and the corner nobody stands in.

Plot every serious product on two axes — how explicitly it teaches the machinery of Irish, and how deeply culture and history are woven into the method — and the market's shape appears at once. The bottom-left is crowded. The top-left is contested. The top-right is empty.

That empty corner is the bet: high structure and high culture at once — explain the grammar school never explained, inside a story worth returning to. blas. proves adults will pay for rigour. Bitesize proves the diaspora responds to identity. Nobody has fused them, because fusing them is an editorial project, not a software project — and editorial is expensive. That cost is the moat, and the risk.

Longbhriseadh — wrecks on the shore

Irish tech keeps drowning in the same four places.

Before imagining new features, walk the tideline. Every wreck below is a real product decision that failed in public — and each one marks a reef that An Turas will sail over too, unless we chart it now.

i

Duolingo replaced native voices with AI

2022–23: native Irish recordings swapped for synthetic audio that blurs the broad/slender distinction — the load-bearing wall of Irish phonology. The community course team walked in August 2024. The Welsh course was mothballed outright, stranding 675,000 learners.

Chart markingBad audio at scale is worse than silence. Nothing ships without native-speaker review — and Duolingo's drop-outs are our funnel only if we are visibly the anti-slop option.
ii

Gamification opened apps, not mouths

A million-plus started Irish on streaks and XP; the mid-funnel collapsed exactly where Irish gets Irish — mutations and the copula. Learners report shame about broken streaks, not about unspoken Irish. Wrong emotion, wrong language.

Chart markingExtrinsic loops sustain app-opens, not identity growth. But note the hard part: nobody has yet proven narrative sustains a daily habit either. That's our experiment to run.
iii

Culture as marketing, not editorial

“Heritage” in the Instagram copy, an AI chat wrapper in the product. Indistinguishable from a hundred other wrappers; barely a hundred downloads. Meanwhile Chineasy — the closest analogue to “beautiful visual world + language” — drew complaints of story without testing, progression without diagnosis.

Chart markingThe culture moat requires a historian and a pedagogue signing every chapter — and narrative must stay coupled to retrieval, or it becomes television.
iv

Pronunciation theatre & platform dependency

Irish speech recognition is research-grade; apps that score pronunciation accept nonsense audio, and learners eventually notice. Memrise phased out the community decks an ecosystem depended on. Academic tools (An Scéalaí, WordBricks) never crossed into consumer retention.

Chart markingEcho-ungraded is honest; fake scores erode trust. Own the content as data, own the audio pipeline, depend on no one's platform for the curriculum.

An Rud atá Tógtha — what the slice already does

Three registers and a chisel: the vertical slice, working.

Chapter 1 — Clocha na nAinmneacha, a stone-carver's workshop on the Mayo coast, c. 480 AD — is running on the iOS simulator today. Its design language has three registers: the scene (illustrated story, spoken Irish as the hero primitive), the note (what school never explained, purely typographic), and the exercise (chalk on the same page, no grey card). An Rud a d'Fhéadfadh a Bheith — what could be

If the thesis holds, the app becomes a place.

Everything below follows from one idea: the retention engine is a world — people, places, artifacts, seasons — and every feature either deepens the world or doesn't ship. Léarscáil na nGnéithe — the feature map

Every feature earns its place on two axes — or pairs with one that does.

Plot our own features the way we plotted the market: how hard each one pulls a learner back (identity gravity, not notification pressure) against how much Irish it actually teaches per minute. Size is the cost to build well. Three regions matter.

The drill corner (top-left) teaches efficiently but pulls weakly — blas. lives there, and our exercises visit it. The museum corner (bottom-right) pulls hard but teaches little — Chineasy drowned there. The spine is the diagonal where pull and yield reinforce. The design rule that falls out: a feature may sit off the diagonal only if it's welded to a partner that completes it — the músaem is legitimate only because Ar Ais wires it to retrieval; the amárach hook is legitimate only because it opens onto a scene with real language inside.

Na Teannais — the trade-offs that decide everything

Where the stone sits on each line — and what would move it.

Six tensions run under every decision in this document. For each: where An Turas currently stands (the stone), why, and the concrete evidence that would make us slide it. A position you can't imagine moving isn't a position — it's a blind spot.

Meaning

Mechanism

Where we standRituals, yes — recarve, amárach hooks, tá tú ar ais, a real-calendar féilire. Guilt, never: no streaks, no hearts, no expiring flames. The wager is that wanting to know what happens beats fearing what breaks.

What would move the stoneIf the playtest shows strong session-one love but weak session-two return (<⅓ coming back unprompted), we add gentler structure — a daily seanfhocal notification, opt-in — before we ever add a streak. Duolingo proves mechanism works on opens; we only borrow from it downhill of meaning.

Depth

Burn

Where we standAuthored everything: narrative by writers, grammar by pedagogues, history signed by a historian, illustration in one canonical style (Solas an Atlantaigh), audio native-reviewed. Cost per learner-hour is orders of magnitude above Duolingo's templates — that is the product.

What would move the stoneChapter 2 through the pipeline is the test: if a chapter costs more than ~6 weeks fully loaded, we hybridise — generation for first drafts, humans for sign-off — and chase the public money (Foras na Gaeilge, Údarás) that exists for exactly this. Quality floor never moves; who does the first 80% can.

Story gates language

Parallel tracks

Where we standLanguage effort unlocks story — pages beyond an unsolved exercise don't exist yet — but review is always dressed as revisiting, never as debt. The story is the carrot; the carrot must never become the paywall for people who came for the history.

What would move the stonePlaytest resentment signals: if testers report skimming exercises to “get back to the story,” the gate is working as pacing. If they report quitting at the gate, we loosen it — story-only mode with language woven lighter — and accept the drilling engine weakens.

The hand

The machine

Where we standPragmatic hybrid, honestly labelled: Gemini TTS for playtest clips (best of a weak field — ElevenLabs and others rejected on pronunciation), ABAIR licensing in motion for dialect fidelity, human narrative voice as the Phase 2+ goal. Illustration: one style bible, generation inside it, human judgment over every frame. Echo stays ungraded — no pronunciation theatre.

What would move the stoneToward the hand: any native-speaker reviewer flags phonology errors in shipped clips — we pull audio that day (silence beats slop). Toward the machine: ABAIR bundling rights land, or a TTS clears native review on the fada minimal pairs (féar/fear, Seán/sean) at production scale.

Craft on one platform

Reach on every platform

Where we standiOS-native, offline-first, haptics as vocabulary — the chisel strike is part of the pedagogy. The product's claim is “cared-for,” and care shows fastest in native feel. Web and Android wait.

What would move the stoneDiaspora demand is the counterweight: the largest paying segment skews global, and the institutional funders (whose money comes with openness strings) will ask. If waitlist data shows >40% Android, Phase 4 re-plans around a second platform — after the content pipeline, never instead of it.

A door

A flag

Where we standIdentity is the engine, but the app is a door anyone can walk through — the East Belfast Turas project proves Irish can be cross-community, and Chapter 10's two-letters structure builds that humility into the page. NI is the north star we take special care over, not a market we posture toward.

What would move the stoneNothing moves it toward the flag — that end is where the plastic-shamrock wrecks are. What deepens the door: Ulster audio landing (D2's hard prerequisite), advisory voices from Turas and Glór na Móna, and editorial principles for contested chapters written before the chapters need them.

An Cheist Amháin · the one question
“Did anything pull you back for session two?”

Every map above is provisional until ten to twenty real learners answer that. The slice now carries three return mechanics — the weathered stone, the amárach hook, the greeting — precisely so the playtest can tell us which line on the feature map is real. The territory map says the empty field exists. Only the playtest says whether we can live in it.
