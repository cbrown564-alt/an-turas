## Dramatic spine

> Can Gráinne preserve her family, authority, and way of life as Tudor power closes
> around Clew Bay?

## Significant reading / object encounter

**Primary learner sequence (recommended):**

1. **Petition** (July 1593) — maintenance plea; offer to serve against the Queen's
   enemies; pressure framing.
2. **Eighteen articles of interrogatory + answers** (July 1593) — family, marriages,
   maintenance by land and sea, Bingham conflict in her voice.
3. **Draft letter, Queen to Bingham, 6 September 1593** — names sons Morogh/Murrough
   O'Flaherty and Tibbott Bourke, brother Donell O'Piper / Donal na Píopa; companions
   at court; royal instruction to investigate/relieve.

**Place encounter:** Rockfleet Castle (Carraig a Chabhlaigh), Clew Bay inlet; Clare
Island as supporting shore geography.

**Recognition source (pre-crisis):** Sidney's description of the feminine / famous sea
captain with galleys and fighting men.

The approved prototype's interactive name-find should bind to the document that best
reveals kin names in English hand (September draft), with petition/articles preparing
voice and stakes—not three equal scavenger hunts.

## Draft L1 paraphrases (historian must approve)

*Working English for story view. Not final app copy. Bracketed words mark soft claims.*

### Petition (Ep3 prepare / Ep4 open)

> In 1593 Gráinne asked the Queen for enough to live on in the time left to her. She
> offered to surrender certain family lands at the Queen's hands, and asked leave to
> move with sword and fire against the Queen's enemies. Behind the polite plea was a
> harder truth: Bingham's pressure had left her without the maintenance she had long
> taken by land and sea.

### Interrogatory voice (Ep4 prepare)

> Burghley sent eighteen written questions. Her answers name her father Dubhdara, once
> chief in Upper Owles; her marriages; her children; and how she had maintained herself
> and her people. She tells the court that discord among chieftains had forced her to
> take arms, and that Bingham's officers had seized, bound, and stripped her of goods
> [when she tried to move under his command]. The answers are her voice shaped for
> English paperwork—not a private diary.

### September draft / kin names (Ep4 climax)

> A draft of the Queen's letter to Bingham, dated 6 September 1593, places Gráinne in
> London with companions and names the kin at stake: her sons Morogh O'Flaherty and
> Tibbott Bourke, and her brother Donell O'Piper. The English hand writes her as
> Grany Ne Maly. Finding those names is how the learner meets the record.

### Royal answer (Ep5)

> Before September was out, the Queen ordered Bingham to release the imprisoned kin and
> to see that Gráinne had maintenance from her sons' estates, living in peace. Bingham
> did not hurry to obey. Relief existed on paper while force on the coast still answered
> to him.

### Afterlife (Ep6)

> Later stories tell of Gráinne refusing to bow to Elizabeth. The letter holds its own
> act of defiance: she crossed from Clew Bay to the heart of English power and made the
> state answer her.
