## 3. Style Analysis & Day 1 Cut

### B3 · Cló-ghearrtha (The Printmaker's Ireland) — **SURVIVOR**
- **Pros**: Outstanding visual personality. The woodcut/linocut line directly mirrors the app's ogham carving mechanic (cutting away material). It feels physical and honest. Printmaking has represented every century in Irish history (from early prints to modern Belfast wall gables), meaning the era-stretch is incredibly natural. Meets the "Killala wall test" perfectly.
- **Cons**: Needs careful framing/vignetting to avoid looking like a generic historical textbook asset.

### B4 · Solas an Atlantaigh (Atlantic Light) — **SURVIVOR**
- **Pros**: Extremely powerful emotional narrative hook. The vast moody Irish sky and stormy coast immediately build atmospheric connection. The soft edges and washes feel organic and artistic. Jack B. Yeats-style characters give the best human close-ups (T2 is exceptionally moving).
- **Cons**: Soft edges contrast sharply with the app's crisp carved UI lines. Dark mode inversion is difficult without muddying the colors. High pipeline cost.

### B2 · Cailc is deannach (Chalk & Dust) — *Runner-Up*
- **Pros**: The loose slate-grey charcoal sketch matches the "chalk guide-marks before carving" metaphor perfectly. Natural fit in dark mode.
- **Cons**: Low color palette makes long-term engagement a concern; it can feel unfinished compared to the other rich visual options.

### Eliminated Directions
- **B1 · Gearrtha**: Too rigid and monochromatic. It is too difficult to stretch this style to modern eras or warm character expressions.
- **B5 · Scáthchruthach**: Fails the "Killala wall test". It feels generic, commercial, and like a standard language learning app.
- **B6 · Lámhscríbhinn**: Anachronistic for Chapter 1 and risks slipping into Celtic kitsch.
